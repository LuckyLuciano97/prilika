Every property sold at judicial auction in Croatia, in enforcement (ovrha) and bankruptcy (stečaj) proceedings, is published in one official public registry run by FINA, the Croatian state financial agency. Prilika reads that registry every day, removes all personal data, and turns it into a searchable site: about 2,400 active listings at any moment, from apartments in Zagreb and Split to Istrian farmland, with the discount against the court-appraised value computed for each one.

Why the prices are lower

Croatian judicial auctions run in rounds. The first round typically opens at or near the full appraised value. If nobody bids, the next round opens lower, often at 50%, then 25% of the appraisal. Across the whole registry the average opening price currently sits about a third below the official appraisal, and repeated auctions regularly reach discounts of 50 to 75%. The discount is real, but so is the reason for it, as explained below.

Can foreigners buy?

EU/EEA citizens can acquire Croatian real estate under essentially the same conditions as Croatian citizens (agricultural land was opened to EU nationals in 2023; a few categories such as protected areas have special rules). Non-EU citizens generally need reciprocity between Croatia and their country plus consent from the Croatian Ministry of Justice. Verify your specific case with a Croatian lawyer before bidding.

What you need in practice

  • A Croatian tax number (OIB), issued to foreigners on request. Any transaction requires it.
  • Registration for FINA's electronic auction system, where bidding takes place. Foreign buyers usually handle registration and bidding through a local lawyer or authorised representative.
  • A deposit (jamčevina) paid in advance, as a rule around one tenth of the appraised value. The exact amount and deadline are set in the conclusion of sale for each item.

The risks to check before bidding

The single most important document is the conclusion of sale (zaključak o prodaji). It states exactly what is being sold and under which conditions. The classic traps behind large discounts: you may be buying a co-ownership share rather than the whole property; some registered encumbrances are extinguished by the sale while others survive; the property may not be vacant; everything is sold as-is, and a viewing is not always possible. The proceedings, documents and bidding interface are in Croatian, so budget for professional help.

How to use Prilika

The site is in Croatian (your browser's translate function works well on it). Start from the full list of active properties, filter by county (Istria, Kvarner and Dalmatia are where most foreign buyers look), or browse the biggest discounts. Every listing links back to the official registry with the auction ID, so you can verify everything at the source before involving your lawyer. Prilika is an independent data service: it is not FINA, not a court, and not a party to any sale.
